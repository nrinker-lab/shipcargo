    🐉 Ming China (1368–1644)

    • China was the richest country in the world. After Zheng He's voyages (1405–1433), China closed itself off. This is called isolationism.
    • Visitors could only trade in Macau (Portugal, 1557) or Canton (Britain, 1600s).

    🔍 Look out for

    • ⚠️ Any European ship. It must stay in Macau or Canton and be back on board by night.
    • 🚫 Gold as payment. China only took silver.
    • 🚫 Opium. The big opium trade didn't start until the 1700s.
    • 🚫 A letter refusing to kowtow (bow low). That didn't happen until 1793.
    • 🚫 Zheng He's treasure fleet after 1433. Those voyages had stopped.

    ⛩️ Japan (1543–1850s)

    • Portugal arrived in 1543 with guns and Christianity. Jesuit missionaries converted over 100,000 people.
    • Starting in 1633, Japan closed its borders to almost everyone.

    🔍 Look out for

    • 🚫 Jesuits, rosaries, or Christian books. Christianity was illegal.
    • 🚫 Portuguese, Spanish, or English ships in 1635. Only the Dutch could trade.
    • ⚠️ Dutch ships. They are allowed, but only in Nagasaki and only if they don't spread their religion.
    • 🚫 Japanese passengers sailing to other countries. Leaving Japan was against the law.

    🕌 Mughal India (1498–1858)

    • Babur started the Mughal Empire in 1526. His grandson Akbar let people choose their religion and made the empire rich.
    • The British East India Company came in 1600. Britain slowly took over and ruled India by 1858.

    🔍 Look out for

    • ✅ Silver, horses, and goods for trade. The Mughals welcomed traders who asked permission.
    • 🚫 A British East India Company ship before 1600. The company didn't exist yet.
    • 🚫 Chili peppers, tomatoes, or corn before 1492. They come from the Americas.
    • ✅ Chili peppers after 1500. The Portuguese really did bring them to India.

    🌿 Spice Islands (Southeast Asia)

    • The only place in the world with nutmeg and cloves.
    • Portugal arrived first (1512). Then the Dutch East India Company (VOC, 1602) took over with its own army and forts. This is called a sphere of influence.

    🔍 Look out for

    • 🚫 A ship bringing spices to the Spice Islands. That's backwards!
    • 🚫 A Dutch VOC ship before 1602. The company didn't exist yet.
    • ⚠️ English or Portuguese ships after 1602. The Dutch attacked their rivals.

    🌍 West Africa (1444–1600s)

    • At first, Europeans traded guns and alcohol for African gold, ivory, and jewels.
    • Portugal started the slave trade in 1444. Europeans used chattel slavery: people were treated as property for life because of their race. About 12.5 million Africans were taken.

    🔍 Look out for

    • ✅ Guns, alcohol, and cloth traded for gold and ivory. This really happened.
    • 🚫 Tobacco, corn, or peanuts before 1492. They come from the Americas.
    • 🚫 A Dutch or British East India Company ship in the 1480s. Those companies started around 1600.

    🌎 The Americas (1492–1607)

    • Columbus landed in 1492. Cortés beat the Aztecs (1521) and Pizarro beat the Incas (1533) with guns, horses, and smallpox.
    • Disease killed about 9 out of 10 Native people. Spain forced survivors to work under the Encomienda System (1502).

    🔍 Look out for

    • ☣️ Smallpox, measles, or influenza. These sicknesses were new to the Americas.
    • 🚫 Potatoes, tomatoes, corn, or cocoa coming FROM Europe. They already grew here!
    • 🚫 Coffee (about 1720) or honeybees (1622). Too early for the 1500s.
    • 🚫 An encomienda contract before 1502, or a Treaty of Tordesillas map before 1494.
    • ✅ Horses, cattle, pigs, wheat, and sugarcane after 1493. These really came.

    🔄 Columbian Exchange

    If a ship from Europe brings tomatoes or potatoes to the Americas in 1492, that's ahistorical. Those foods already grew there! The years show when each item crossed the ocean.
